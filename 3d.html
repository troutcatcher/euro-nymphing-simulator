How to fish it

Cast by moving the rod. Sweep the tip up and upstream and the weight of the flies carries them out; drop the tip and they drop in and start sinking. There is no cast button — the leader is a real rope and you are throwing it.

Lead the sighter downstream at the speed of the water. Contact wants to sit in the marked band: tight enough that a take moves the sighter, loose enough that you are not towing the flies.

Get down. Fish eat within about 30 cm of the bed. Deeper or faster water needs a heavier bead, a longer leader, or a cast further upstream.

Strike at anything the sighter does that the current did not do — a stall, a tick, a lift. You have a few tenths of a second. Sweep the rod tip up (or up and downstream) to set the hook, or use click / Space. A slow raise through the drift will not trip it; a sharp one will.

Play it by holding the pointer down to gather line and releasing to give it back. Watch the tippet load.

On a phone or tablet: drag to move the rod, flick up to set, and press and hold to gather line while you steer.

R drops the rig back under the tip if you tangle it or want to start again.

Keys: Space gather line · R reset the rig · C tuck cast (fallback) · F full screen · M sound · L learning mode · P pause · 1–5 beats

Sound (M, or the speaker) is the river itself plus the things worth hearing — the flies going in, a bead knocking stone, line tearing off a running fish. Nothing marks a take: you cannot hear a trout eat.

Full screen (F, or the ⤢ button) hides this panel and gives the river the whole screen, with the meters you need moved to the top edge. Best held sideways.
